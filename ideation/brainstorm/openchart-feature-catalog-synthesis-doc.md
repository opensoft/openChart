## Focus

This synthesis shows how the 70 DOC-domain capabilities join into an encounter-centered clinical record. The shared design keeps provenance, consent, human signature, and succession-based amendment intact across every care setting and authoring surface.

## Frappe realization

- **DocType graph:** `OC Clinical Encounter` anchors submittable notes, observations, assessments, signatures, release records, and transition artifacts through stable Links and child tables; configuration DocTypes are versioned and snapshotted at use.
- **Workflow and succession:** Frappe Workflows separate drafting, review, acceptance, release, and closure; accepted clinical records amend only through `open_chart.api.v1` successor creation, never in-place edits.
- **Roles and permissions:** Clinical, nursing, supervisory, health-information, privacy, portal, and mobile roles use DocPerms, permlevels, user permissions, and purpose-of-use checks to constrain every surface.
- **Hooks and APIs:** `doc_events`, scheduler events, background jobs, realtime events, and versioned whitelisted APIs coordinate validation, queues, escalation, audit, indexing, and guarded integration.
- **Surfaces:** Desk workspaces, Query and Script Reports, client scripts, Jinja Print Formats/PDF, authenticated portal pages and Web Forms, and offline-capable mobile forms expose task-appropriate views.

## Boundaries

Owns: encounter-centered clinical documentation, acceptance state, provenance, correction lineage, and controlled renditions. Consumes: patient identity, appointments, consent, terminology, results, orders, and facility context through authorized references. Emits: accepted clinical records, stable evidence links, disclosures, audit events, and permissioned integration payloads. Does not own: scheduling operations, claims adjudication, autonomous diagnosis or ordering, identity merge policy, or external exchange governance.

## Emergent behavior

Together, the members create a closed documentation loop: an encounter begins with traceable context, contributors author evidence-bearing records, humans review and sign them, follow-up artifacts leave through controlled channels, and later corrections preserve the complete lineage. Operational queues can reveal missing work without becoming a second clinical source of truth.

## Tensions to hold

- Structured capture improves reuse but must not force false certainty or imply an assessment was performed.
- Fast defaults, templates, copy-forward, dictation, and AI drafts reduce effort but must remain visibly sourced and actively reviewed.
- Broad team collaboration must coexist with section-level authorship, sensitive-note restrictions, and explicit signature meaning.
- Patient access and portable exports must preserve transparency while honoring lawful holds, consent, proxy scope, and corrected-record notices.
- Offline and realtime workflows must never trade silent conflict or unverifiable signatures for speed.

## Recombination opportunities

- Encounter profiles, documentation policies, templates, and completeness checks could become a configurable specialty documentation kit.
- Timeline, chart search, evidence insertion, and governed drafting could form a cited clinical context workspace.
- Flowsheets, risk assessments, reassessment timers, and SBAR handoffs could form a bedside continuity surface.
- Signature requests, deficiencies, overdue escalation, release, and audit could form a health-information operations console.
